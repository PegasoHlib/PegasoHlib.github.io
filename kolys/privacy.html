Kolys

Privacy policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

Kolys is an Android app for writing letters to your future self (“time capsules”). This policy explains what the app does with your data. In short: your letters stay on your phone and, if you turn it on, in your own Google Drive. Gifts, group capsules and letter files are files you send yourself, and this page says plainly how they are locked.

At a glance

  • Your letters, photos, videos and voice messages are kept on your phone.
  • Kolys has no servers of its own. The developer never receives your letters, photos or any other data.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no selling of data. Location is used only when you ask for the city and the weather, only roughly, and is not stored.
  • Google Drive is optional and reaches only the app’s own hidden folder.
  • A sealed letter is locked by the app, not encrypted. Anyone who gets past Android’s app protection could read it, and Google could read the Drive copy.
  • Gifts, group capsules and letter files are files you send yourself. The developer never receives them, but they carry the “moment”, with the phone’s name or model and battery level.

What stays on your phone

The app stores on your phone:

  • your capsules and drafts: name, letter text, the date written, the opening date, the seal colour, whether it has been opened, and which earlier letter it answers;
  • copies of the photos and videos you add (photos made smaller), and the voice messages you record;
  • “this moment”: your mood, a song, the city and the weather if you add them, and always the phone’s name (the one you gave it, or else its maker and model) and the battery level;
  • capsules that friends send you and, for a group capsule you organize, the letters collected so far;
  • your settings (language, theme, your birthday if you use it for a capsule, the connected Google account’s e-mail).

Photos and videos are chosen through Android’s own photo picker, so the app sees only the ones you pick, never your whole gallery. The microphone is used only while you are recording a voice message. All of this is kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read, but the files themselves are not encrypted. The app turns off Android’s automatic backup. Photos and videos you take in Kolys are taken by your phone’s camera app; Kolys itself has no camera permission.

How a sealed letter is protected

A sealed letter is locked by the app: Kolys does not open or show it before its day, and the date is checked online. This is a lock in the app, not encryption.

On your phone, letters, photos and recordings are ordinary files and database entries in the app’s private storage. Someone who gets past Android’s app protection, for example on a phone that is rooted or opened with special tools, could read them. The zip in your Google Drive is not encrypted either, so Google could read it, as it can anything stored on Drive.

Gift files and group capsules work differently, see “Capsules as gifts” below. Do not put into a capsule anything that must stay unreadable to others.

Google Drive (optional)

If you sign in with Google, Kolys asks for one permission: drive.appdata. It lets the app create, read and delete only its own files in a hidden app folder on your Google Drive. The app cannot see or change any other files in your Drive.

Each sealed capsule is saved there as one ordinary zip file named with the capsule’s random id (the letter, its photos, videos, voice messages and “this moment”), not encrypted. The opening date and the date it was opened are also kept as properties of the file. Drafts are not uploaded. A sync starts a few seconds after a change when there is internet, and about twice a day. This is what brings your sealed capsules back on a new phone. The app also reads your account’s e-mail address only to show you which account is connected.

Data travels directly between your phone and Google over an encrypted connection. There is no other server in between. Google’s own privacy policy applies to what is stored in your Drive.

How Google data is used

Kolys’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • Data from your Google Drive is used only to keep your capsules and bring them back to your phones.
  • It is never sold, never used for ads and never shared with anyone.
  • No person reads it: the developer has no access to your Drive or your letters.

Checking the date

So that a capsule can’t be opened early by moving the phone’s clock, the app asks a web server for the current time (www.google.com or www.cloudflare.com) and reads the date from the reply. It asks again when a capsule’s day comes, before it tells you. Nothing about you or your capsules is sent. As with any web request, these services see your IP address.

City and weather (optional)

Only when you tap “Add the city and the weather” while writing, the app asks Android for your approximate location (never the precise one) once. The phone turns it into a city name, and the app asks MET Norway (Norwegian Meteorological Institute, api.met.no) for the current weather, sending the location rounded to about a kilometre. Only the city name and the weather are kept in the capsule; the location itself is not stored. You can always leave this out.

Song search (optional)

When you look for the song of the moment, the words you type are sent to MusicBrainz (musicbrainz.org), an open music database, to find matching song names. Only the song’s name, artist and year are kept in the capsule, never covers or recordings. “Listen” passes the song’s name to the music app on your phone (or opens a YouTube Music search), and that app works under its own rules. You can also just type a song yourself without searching.

Capsules as gifts

A capsule for someone else becomes one file that you send yourself, in the app you choose (a messenger, e-mail, Google Drive). The developer never receives it. The file holds the letter with its photos, videos, voice messages and “this moment” (with the phone’s name or model and the battery level), and your name if you give it. The file’s name contains the name you typed for the recipient or the group, which the messenger or mail service can see.

Without a secret word the file is encrypted, but the key is stored inside the file. This stops a friend from unpacking it early by accident, not anyone who has the file and some skill. If you lock it with a secret word, the key is made from the word and is in no file, so the file is as safe as the word is hard to guess. The word stays only on your phone, so you can look it up again, and never goes into the file, your Google Drive or to the developer. Only the hint you write can be read before unlocking.

A capsule for a group also carries the group’s name and the names you list. When a group writes a capsule together, the invite file holds the group’s key unencrypted, so anyone who has the invite can read the letters sent back. Each person’s letter goes as a file to the one collecting them, encrypted with that key. Until the capsule is sealed, the collected letters sit as ordinary files in the organizer’s app storage; the app shows only who has written, not what, and they are not copied to Drive.

Once the recipient opens the file in Kolys, their copy lives in their app, unencrypted like any capsule, and, if they use it, in their own Google Drive. Files you have sent stay with the recipients and in the messenger or mail service; deleting a capsule in Kolys cannot take them back.

Notifications, calendar and widget

The notification on the opening day is scheduled on your phone and waits for an internet connection, because the date is checked first; nothing about it is sent anywhere. “Add to calendar” opens your phone’s calendar app with a ready event (the capsule’s name and opening date). You decide whether to save it, and it is then kept by your calendar app. Kolys cannot read your calendar. The home-screen widget shows your nearest capsule’s name and the days left; the large one shows up to three capsules with a progress bar and the opening dates.

Updates

If automatic checks are on (they are by default), the version of Kolys from this website checks the Kolys website (pegasohlib.github.io) for a new version when you open the app, at most once every 30 minutes. There is no check in the background. It downloads the update from GitHub only when you tap Update, and checks the file’s SHA-256 before installing. Like any website, GitHub sees the IP address of your device; nothing else is sent, and no data from the app.

You can turn automatic checks off in Settings. When the app comes from Google Play, Google Play updates it instead.

GitHub also counts how many times each file has been downloaded. It is only a number, with nothing about who downloaded it; the website shows it as an approximate number of downloads.

What Kolys does not do

  • no accounts with the developer and no servers of its own;
  • no analytics, no ads, no tracking, no crash reports sent anywhere;
  • no selling of data, and nothing sent to the developer;
  • no access to your contacts, files or gallery, and no location in the background.

Deleting your data

  • Deleting a capsule in the app removes the letter, its files, “this moment”, its reminders and the saved gift word from your phone at once, whether or not you are signed in. Only a blank row with the capsule’s random id stays until the next sync. Leftovers of older versions are blanked when the new version starts.
  • On Google Drive the capsule’s zip is deleted, and a small file with the capsule’s id and the ending .gone stays. It holds only the word “gone” and tells your other phones to delete the capsule too. A capsule you changed on another phone since then stays. A reply keeps its own copy of the letters it answers, so they survive deleting the original.
  • Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored on the phone, but not the copy in your Google Drive and not the files you sent to others.
  • To remove the Drive copy without the app: open Google Drive on the web → Settings → Manage apps → Kolys → Options → Delete hidden app data.
  • You can take away the app’s access at any time at myaccount.google.com/connections.

Children

Kolys collects no personal data from anyone, children included. It has no accounts and no chat; a file you send to someone is sent by you.
